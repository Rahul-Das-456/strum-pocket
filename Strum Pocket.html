Your hand never stops: down on the number, up on the “&”. Tap a slot to switch it between strum, ghost and air.

What is a ghost strum?

A ghost strum keeps your hand swinging while your fretting hand relaxes onto the strings, so you hear a soft, percussive chk instead of a ringing chord. An air strum swings over the strings without touching them. Ghosts add rhythm; air keeps the motion steady when you want silence.

Song sheet

Paste a song here from a tab page, or type your own. Chords can sit above the words, or inline in brackets like [C]word. Add a pipe and a number after a chord to set its length in beats, like C|8. Lines such as Verse or Chorus become headings.

Default chord length

The highlight moves on every chord change, counted from the end of the count-in. The Yellow skeleton has chords only, because the lyrics are not included here. Microphone and latency
Mic check

Strums and ghosts should push the level bar past the orange line, and the dot beside it lights up whenever a hit is heard. Raise sensitivity if ghosts are missed, lower it if the room is triggering hits.

Where does the click play?

On speaker, the click leaks into the mic. Quiet leaks are ignored and loud strums still count, but headphones give the cleanest reading.

Take headphones off, set the volume to medium-loud and put the speaker near the mic. Strum Pocket plays ten clicks and measures how long they take to come back, so a strum exactly on the click reads as 0 ms. Bluetooth headphones add extra delay; use the slider to nudge it.
